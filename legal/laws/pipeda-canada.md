# PIPEDA + Quebec Law 25: Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (S.C. 2000, c. 5) and the Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector (CQLR c. P-39.1), as modernized by 2021, c. 25

| **In force since** | PIPEDA assented 2000-04-13. PIPEDA mandatory breach reporting in force 2018-11-01 (SI/2018-32). Law 25 staged: 2022-09-22 (privacy officer, incident reporting), 2023-09-22 (the bulk), 2024-09-22 (data portability). All three dates are set by section 175 of S.Q. 2021, c. 25 |

Two laws, one file, because a Canadian client almost always triggers both and the Quebec rules are the ones that change the build.

Rule of thumb for the studio: **build to Law 25 and you have covered PIPEDA.** Law 25 is stricter on consent, adds obligations PIPEDA has no equivalent for (privacy officer published on the site, privacy impact assessment, portability, automated decision disclosure), and is the only one of the two with real fines.

---

## Applies when

Answer these at intake. Work the PIPEDA block and the Law 25 block separately, because a client can be caught by one, the other, or both.

### PIPEDA triggers

- [ ] Is the client a private-sector organization that collects, uses, or discloses personal information **in the course of commercial activity**? That is the core test in [section 4(1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html). A commercial activity is any transaction, act, or conduct of a commercial character.
- [ ] Is the client a **federally regulated** business (bank, airline, airport, telecom carrier, broadcaster, interprovincial rail or trucking)? Those are always subject to PIPEDA, and PIPEDA also covers their **employee** personal information ([OPC, PIPEDA in brief](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/)). For everyone else, PIPEDA does not reach employee data.
- [ ] Will personal information **cross a provincial or national border** in the course of commercial activity? If yes, PIPEDA applies regardless of which province the client sits in, including Quebec, Alberta, and BC ([OPC, PIPEDA in brief](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/)). US hosting, a US CRM, or a US analytics vendor makes this a yes for practically every site you will build.

**There is no revenue threshold, no employee-count threshold, and no record-count threshold in PIPEDA.** A sole proprietor with a contact form is in scope.

**Who is not caught by PIPEDA**

- Organizations in Quebec, Alberta, or BC, for personal information collected, used, and disclosed **wholly within that province**. Those three provinces have laws declared substantially similar, so the intra-provincial activity is exempt under [section 26(2)(b)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html). The provincial law applies instead. The moment data leaves the province, PIPEDA is back.
- Federal government institutions. They are under the Privacy Act, not PIPEDA.
- Personal or domestic use, and journalistic, artistic, or literary purposes.

**Foreign organizations, including a US studio or a US client**

PIPEDA reaches organizations outside Canada where there is a **real and substantial connection** to Canada. The OPC applied this in [PIPEDA Report of Findings #2015-002 (Globe24h)](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-actions-and-decisions/investigations/investigations-into-businesses/2015/pipeda-2015-002/), against a Romanian-operated site, and the Federal Court upheld the approach. Practical factors: does the business market to Canadians, does it process Canadians' personal information, would misuse of that information affect Canadians.

### Law 25 (Quebec) triggers

- [ ] Does the client **carry on an enterprise in Quebec**? Section 1 of [P-39.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1) attaches the Act to a person who collects, holds, uses, or communicates personal information "in the course of carrying on an enterprise within the meaning of article 1525 of the Civil Code".
- [ ] Does the site collect personal information **from people in Quebec**, even if the client is elsewhere? Treat this as a yes-trigger and build to Law 25. See the caution below.
- [ ] Will personal information be **communicated outside Quebec**, or will a person or body outside Quebec collect, use, communicate, or keep it on the client's behalf? That is [section 17](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1) and it fires on US hosting, US SaaS, and on the studio itself if the studio is outside Quebec.
- [ ] Is the project an **acquisition, development, or overhaul of an information system or electronic service delivery system** involving personal information? That is [section 3.3](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1) and it makes a privacy impact assessment mandatory. A new website with a contact form or an account system meets this description.

**There is no revenue threshold, no headcount threshold, and no record-count threshold in Law 25 either.** The obligations scale by proportionality (the assessment must be proportionate to sensitivity, purpose, quantity, distribution, and medium, section 3.3 last paragraph), but they do not switch off for small businesses.

**Cautions on scope, flagged honestly**

- **Article 1525 of the Civil Code of Québec is broader than PIPEDA's commercial-activity test.** Third paragraph, verbatim from LégisQuébec: "The carrying on by one or more persons of an organized economic activity, whether or not it is commercial in nature, consisting of producing, administering or alienating property, or providing a service, constitutes the operation of an enterprise." Non-commercial organized economic activity counts, so a Quebec nonprofit or association running an organized service can be caught by Law 25 where PIPEDA would not reach it. Do not tell a Quebec nonprofit it is out of scope on the strength of the PIPEDA analysis alone.
- **P-39.1 contains no express extraterritoriality clause.** Unlike CASL, there is no "accessed in Quebec" hook written into the statute. Whether the Act binds a business with no Quebec establishment that simply has Quebec customers is a question counsel argue about, see [Fasken, "The Québec Private Sector Privacy Act: When does it Apply to Organizations Outside of Québec?"](https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2019/05/van-the-quebec-private-sector-privacy-act) (secondary source). Studio position: if the client markets to Quebec, build to Law 25. The delta in build cost is small and the downside is a 2% of worldwide turnover penalty.

### How a US studio building for a Canadian client gets caught

The studio is almost never the "organization" or the "person carrying on an enterprise" for the client's data. The studio is the processor. That still creates three concrete obligations that land in your contract and your build:

1. **The client stays accountable for what you hold.** PIPEDA [Schedule 1, clause 4.1.3](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html): "An organization is responsible for personal information in its possession or custody, including information that has been transferred to a third party for processing. The organization shall use contractual or other means to provide a comparable level of protection." Expect the client to push a data protection addendum at you. Sign one that you can actually honour.
2. **Quebec requires the mandate to be in writing with specified measures.** [Section 18.3](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1) lets a Quebec enterprise hand personal information to a service provider without consent only if the mandate or contract is **in writing** and **specifies the measures** the provider must take to protect confidentiality, to use the information only for the contract, and to **not keep the information after the contract expires**. That last one is a real deliverable: your offboarding has to delete client-side personal information, including from your staging environments, backups, and error logs.
3. **You notify the client's privacy officer on any incident.** Same section 18.3: the person performing the contract must notify the client's person in charge of the protection of personal information of any violation or attempted violation of the confidentiality obligations. Put that escalation path in the runbook you hand over, with a name and an address.

Separately, if the client is in Quebec and you (the studio), your hosting, or your subprocessors are outside Quebec, the client owes a **section 17 privacy impact assessment plus a written agreement** before any personal information moves. You will be asked for the vendor list. Have it ready.

## What the site must have

| Requirement | What that means in the build |
|---|---|
| **Privacy policy page, published, plain language** | PIPEDA openness principle [4.8.1 to 4.8.2](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html) requires the name or title and address of the accountable person, the means of gaining access, a description of the types of personal information held and a general account of its use, and what is made available to related organizations. Quebec [section 8.2](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1) requires anyone collecting personal information through technological means to **publish a confidentiality policy on the website in clear and simple language**, and to publish a notice for any amendment to it. Ship `/privacy` (and `/confidentialite` for a French build), linked from the global footer on every page. |
| **Named privacy officer, title and contact published on the site** | Quebec [section 3.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): the person exercising the highest authority in the enterprise is by default the person in charge of the protection of personal information and may delegate in writing. "The title and contact information of the person in charge of the protection of personal information must be published on the enterprise's website or, if the enterprise does not have a website, be made available by any other appropriate means." For a site build the website limb is the one that applies, and it is a build blocker. Do not launch a Quebec client with a placeholder. Get a title (it may be a title rather than a name) and a monitored address, and render it on the privacy page. |
| **Governance policies published, not only written** | Quebec [section 3.2](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): the enterprise must establish governance policies covering retention and destruction, staff roles across the information life cycle, and a complaints process. **Detailed information about those policies must be published in simple and clear language on the website.** In practice this is a second published document or a long section of the privacy page. A one-paragraph privacy notice does not satisfy section 3.2. |
| **Purpose notice at the point of collection** | Quebec [section 8](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1) requires that at collection you inform the person of the purposes, the means of collection, the rights of access and rectification, and **the right to withdraw consent**. Second paragraph, where applicable and without waiting to be asked: the name of the third person for whom the information is collected, the third persons or categories of third persons it must be communicated to, and the possibility that it could be communicated outside Quebec. Third paragraph, on request only: the personal information collected from the person, the categories of people inside the enterprise with access, how long it is kept, and the contact information of the person in charge. Build it as a short notice next to the submit button, linking to the full policy. PIPEDA principle 4.2 wants the same thing. |
| **Consent that is per-purpose and presented separately** | Quebec [section 14](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): consent "must be clear, free and informed and be given for specific purposes. It must be requested for each such purpose, in clear and simple language. If the request for consent is made in writing, it must be presented separately from any other information." Consequence for the build: one bundled "I agree to the Terms and Privacy Policy" checkbox does not carry marketing consent, analytics consent, or profiling consent. Separate control per purpose. Consent not given in accordance with the Act "is without effect" (section 14, fourth paragraph). |
| **Express consent for sensitive information** | Quebec [section 13](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1), second paragraph: consent to communicate sensitive personal information to a third person "must be given expressly". OPC's [meaningful consent guidelines](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/collecting-personal-information/consent/gl_omc_201805/) require express consent where the information is sensitive, the use falls outside reasonable expectations, or there is a meaningful residual risk of significant harm. Health, financial, biometric, and precise location fields need an explicit affirmative action, never a pre-ticked box. |
| **Tracking technology notice with an activation control** | Quebec [section 8.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): if you collect personal information using "technology that includes functions allowing the person concerned to be identified, located or profiled", you must **first** inform the person of the use of the technology and of "the means available to activate the functions". Profiling is defined in the section as collecting and using personal information to assess characteristics such as work performance, economic situation, health, preferences, interests, or behaviour. Build rule: analytics with cross-session identifiers, ad pixels, session replay, heatmaps, and fingerprinting scripts stay **off until the user turns them on**. Granular categories, no pre-checked boxes, reject as easy as accept. |
| **Highest confidentiality by default on product settings** | Quebec [section 9.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): a technological product or service offered to the public that has privacy settings must have those settings at "the highest level of confidentiality by default, without any intervention by the person concerned". Read the second paragraph before you argue about cookie banners: **"The first paragraph does not apply to privacy settings for browser cookies."** So the default-high rule governs your app's own settings (profile visibility, directory listing, discoverability, sharing defaults), and cookie behaviour is governed by section 8.1 and the consent rules instead. New account defaults: private, not indexed, not shared, no email discoverability. |
| **Withdraw consent, on the site, not by phone** | PIPEDA [clause 4.3.8](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html): an individual may withdraw consent at any time subject to legal or contractual restrictions and reasonable notice, and you must inform them of the implications. Quebec section 8 requires you to tell them the right exists. Deliver a settings screen or a working link, plus a re-open control for the consent banner in the footer. |
| **Access request channel routed to the privacy officer** | Quebec [section 30](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): a request must be **in writing** and **addressed to the person in charge of the protection of personal information**. So a generic `info@` inbox is the wrong destination. Ship a form or a dedicated address that lands with the named officer. |
| **30-day response, and silence counts against you** | Quebec [section 32](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): reply in writing "promptly and not later than 30 days after the date the request is received", and "failure to respond within 30 days of the receipt of a request is deemed to be a refusal". PIPEDA [section 8(3) to 8(5)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html): 30 days, extension possible with written notice of the new limit and the reasons, failure to respond is deemed refusal. Build a ticketing path with a 30-day timer, not an inbox someone checks weekly. |
| **Access is free, transcription may be charged with advance notice** | Quebec [section 33](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): access is free of charge, but a reasonable charge may be required for transcription, reproduction, or transmission, and the applicant must be told the approximate amount **in advance**. PIPEDA [section 8(6)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html) is the same pattern. Do not build a paywalled export. |
| **Data portability export (Quebec, live since 2024-09-22)** | Quebec [section 27](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1), third paragraph: "Unless doing so raises serious practical difficulties, computerized personal information **collected from the applicant, and not created or inferred using personal information concerning him**, must, at his request, be communicated to him in a structured, commonly used technological format. The information must also be communicated, at the applicant's request, to any person or body authorized by law to collect such information." Build a JSON or CSV export that covers form submissions, account fields, uploaded content, and preferences. It does **not** have to cover scores, segments, or inferences you derived. It does have to support direct transfer to a third party on request. PIPEDA has **no** portability right, this is Quebec only. |
| **Rectification and de-indexing paths** | Quebec [section 28](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1) (rectify inaccurate, incomplete, or equivocal information, or information collected, communicated, or kept without legal authority) and [section 28.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1) (require an enterprise to cease disseminating information or to de-index a hyperlink attached to the person's name, on stated conditions). If the site publishes user-identifying content (profiles, reviews, directories, testimonials, staff bios), you need an intake path and an actual takedown and de-index procedure, including a `noindex` mechanism and a sitemap update. |
| **Automated decision disclosure** | Quebec [section 12.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): if a decision is based **exclusively** on automated processing, inform the person no later than when you tell them the decision, and on request give the personal information used, "the reasons and the principal factors and parameters that led to the decision", and the right to correct that information. The person must be able to submit observations to a **member of personnel who can review the decision**. This bites on automated pricing, eligibility screens, credit or risk scoring, application triage, and algorithmic content gating. Build a disclosure string on the decision screen plus a human review route. |
| **Minors** | Quebec [section 4.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): personal information about a minor **under 14** may not be collected from them without the consent of the person having parental authority or the tutor, "unless collecting the information is clearly for the minor's benefit". Section 14, second paragraph: under 14, consent comes from the parent or tutor; at 14 and over, the minor or the parent or tutor may consent. OPC's [consent guidelines](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/collecting-personal-information/consent/gl_omc_201805/) say that in all but exceptional circumstances a person **under 13** cannot give meaningful consent. If the audience skews young, cross-reference `coppa.md` and `ca-minors-eraser-button.md`, the thresholds differ per law and you must satisfy all of them. |
| **Security measures proportionate to sensitivity** | Quebec [section 10](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1) and PIPEDA [clause 4.7.3](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html) (physical, organizational, technological, naming passwords and encryption). Minimum for a delivered site: TLS everywhere with HSTS, encryption at rest for the datastore, least-privilege access to the CMS and the form backend, MFA on admin accounts, and no personal information in client-side logs or third-party error trackers without a contract. |
| **Retention and destruction rules that actually run** | Quebec [section 23](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): when the purposes are achieved, destroy the information or anonymize it for serious and legitimate purposes, subject to statutory preservation periods. Anonymization must follow "generally accepted best practices and the criteria and terms determined by regulation" (see the Regulation respecting the anonymization of personal information, CQLR A-2.1, r. 0.1, listed on the [P-39.1 regulations index](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1)). PIPEDA [clause 4.5.3](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html) is the equivalent. Deliver a scheduled purge job with a documented retention period per data type, not a policy sentence. Quebec [section 11](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1) also requires information used to make a decision about a person to be kept for **at least one year** after the decision, so the purge job needs a floor as well as a ceiling. |
| **Confidentiality incident register (Quebec)** | Quebec [section 3.8](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1) requires a register of **all** confidentiality incidents, not only reportable ones, and a copy goes to the CAI on request. The [Regulation respecting confidentiality incidents (O.C. 1761-2022)](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cr/A-2.1,%20r.%203.1), section 7, sets the eight required fields: description of the information involved, circumstances, when it occurred, when the body became aware, number of people affected, the reasoning on risk of serious injury, transmission dates of any notices, and the measures taken. Section 8 of that regulation: keep the register **at least 5 years** after the date the body became aware of the incident. |
| **Breach record (PIPEDA)** | PIPEDA [section 10.3(1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html) requires a record of **every** breach of security safeguards, reportable or not. [Breach of Security Safeguards Regulations, SOR/2018-64, section 6](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2018-64/FullText.html): "an organization must maintain a record of every breach of security safeguards for **24 months** after the day on which the organization determines that the breach has occurred." Two registers, two retention clocks. If the client is in Quebec and data crosses a border, keep one register built to the Quebec fields and the 5-year clock, which satisfies both. |
| **Breach notification wiring** | See the next block. Ship the client a one-page runbook with the two regulator forms bookmarked and the officer's name filled in. |
| **Cross-border transfer file (Quebec)** | Quebec [section 17](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): before communicating personal information outside Quebec, conduct a privacy impact assessment considering sensitivity, purposes, protection measures including contractual ones, and "the legal framework applicable in the State in which the information would be communicated". The information may only go if the assessment establishes adequate protection, and the communication must be covered by a **written agreement** reflecting the assessment results. Same rule when you entrust an outside-Quebec person or body with collecting, using, communicating, or keeping the information. Deliverable from the studio: a vendor inventory listing every third party that touches personal information, its country, what data it gets, and the contract in place. |
| **Project-level privacy impact assessment (Quebec)** | Quebec [section 3.3](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): a PIA is required "for any project to acquire, develop or overhaul an information system or electronic service delivery system involving the collection, use, communication, keeping or destruction of personal information", the person in charge must be consulted **from the outset**, and the project must be built so that computerized personal information collected from the person can be communicated to them "in a structured, commonly used technological format". The portability export is therefore a design requirement of the PIA, not an afterthought. Schedule the PIA in the intake step, not before launch. |

### Breach reporting, side by side

| | PIPEDA | Quebec Law 25 |
|---|---|---|
| Threshold | "Real risk of significant harm" to an individual, [section 10.1(1)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html) | "Risk of serious injury", [section 3.5](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1) |
| Factors | Sensitivity of the information and probability of misuse, [section 10.1(8)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html). Significant harm is defined in 10.1(7) to include bodily harm, humiliation, damage to reputation or relationships, loss of employment or business opportunities, financial loss, identity theft, credit record effects, and loss of property | Sensitivity, anticipated consequences of use, likelihood of injurious use, and the person in charge must be consulted, [section 3.7](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1) |
| Regulator notice | To the OPC "as soon as feasible after the organization determines that the breach has occurred", [section 10.1(2)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html). No fixed hour count. Use the [OPC breach report form](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/report-a-concern/report-a-privacy-breach-at-your-organization/report-a-privacy-breach-at-your-business/) | To the CAI "promptly", [section 3.5](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1). Content is prescribed by [O.C. 1761-2022, section 3](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cr/A-2.1,%20r.%203.1), 11 items including the Quebec business number and the number of affected people **resident in Quebec** |
| Individual notice | Required by [section 10.1(3)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html) on the same real-risk test. Section 10.1(5): conspicuous and given directly in the prescribed form and manner, except in prescribed circumstances. Section 10.1(6): "as soon as feasible after the organization determines that the breach has occurred" | Required, and the CAI may order it if you do not. Content prescribed by [O.C. 1761-2022, section 5](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cr/A-2.1,%20r.%203.1). May be withheld only while notification would hamper a law enforcement investigation |
| Record of all incidents | Yes, 24 months | Yes, register, at least 5 years |

Neither law sets a 72-hour clock. Do not copy a GDPR runbook and tell a Canadian client they have 72 hours, and do not let them believe they have 30 days either. "As soon as feasible" and "promptly" both mean days, and both regulators judge you on the delay.

## How to verify

Run these before delivery. Each one produces an artifact you can paste into the handover.

**Documents and links**

1. `curl -sI https://site/privacy` returns 200. Same for the French route on a Quebec build.
2. Grep the rendered footer partial for the privacy link. It must appear on every template, including checkout, thank-you, error, and 404 pages: `grep -ril "privacy" src/components/footer* src/layouts/*`.
3. Open `/privacy` and confirm, by eye, all of: the **title and contact information of the person in charge** (Quebec section 3.1), the categories of personal information, the purposes, the third parties and their countries, retention periods, the access, rectification, portability, and withdrawal paths, and the complaints route. Missing officer contact is an automatic fail on a Quebec build.
4. Confirm a separate published description of governance policies exists (Quebec section 3.2), covering retention and destruction, staff roles, and the complaints process. A privacy policy alone does not close this.

**Tracking behaviour, before consent**

5. Open the site in a clean incognito profile. DevTools, Application panel, Cookies and Local Storage. Before touching the banner, the only entries permitted are strictly necessary ones (session, CSRF, load balancer, the consent record itself). Screenshot the empty state.
6. DevTools, Network panel, filter by domain. Before consent there must be **no** requests to `google-analytics.com`, `googletagmanager.com` (if GTM fires tags), `doubleclick.net`, `connect.facebook.net`, `hotjar.com`, `clarity.ms`, `segment.io`, or any other identifier-setting third party. Sort by Domain and screenshot the list.
7. Click Reject. Reload. Re-check both panels. Nothing new. A reject that leaves the pixel firing is the single most common failure in this file.
8. Click Accept for one category only. Confirm only that category's scripts fire. Per-purpose consent that turns everything on is not consent under Quebec section 14.
9. Confirm the banner has a Reject control at the same level as Accept, and that the banner can be reopened from a persistent footer link.
10. Check the consent record: `JSON.parse(localStorage.getItem('<your-consent-key>'))` should contain a timestamp, a policy or banner version, and per-purpose booleans. If you cannot show the client what a given user consented to and when, they cannot demonstrate compliance.

**Forms and collection points**

11. For every form on the site, confirm a purpose notice is visible at the point of collection, not buried in a link only. `grep -rn "type=\"email\"\|type=\"tel\"\|name=\"phone\"" src/` and check each hit has a matching notice.
12. Confirm no consent checkbox is pre-checked: `grep -rn "checked" src/**/*consent* src/**/*form*`.
13. Confirm marketing consent is a separate control from terms acceptance. One checkbox covering both is a defect.
14. Submit a test record and confirm the payload sent to each third party. Network panel, click the request, Payload tab. If an email address is going to an analytics endpoint, stop.

**Rights machinery**

15. File a test access request through the published channel and confirm it lands with the named officer, not a generic inbox. Confirm the 30-day timer starts somewhere trackable.
16. Trigger the portability export. Confirm the file parses as JSON or CSV, contains the fields collected from the user, and excludes derived scores or segments. Open it in a text editor and read it. A PDF is not "a structured, commonly used technological format".
17. If the product makes exclusively automated decisions, confirm the decision screen carries the section 12.1 disclosure and a working route to a human who can review.

**Security and defaults**

18. `curl -sI https://site | grep -i "strict-transport-security\|content-security-policy"`. HSTS present, CSP present and not `unsafe-inline` everywhere.
19. Create a fresh account and inspect the default settings row in the database. Everything privacy-related defaults to the most private value (Quebec section 9.1). If the signup flow sets `profile_public = true`, that is a violation.
20. Check the error tracker and server logs for personal information: `grep -riE "[a-z0-9._%+-]+@[a-z0-9.-]+\.[a-z]{2,}" logs/ | head`. Emails in plaintext logs shipped to a US vendor is a section 17 transfer no one assessed.

**Records and vendors**

21. Confirm the incident register exists as a real document or table with the eight fields from O.C. 1761-2022 section 7, and that the client knows where it lives.
22. Produce the vendor inventory: every third party that receives personal information, its country, the data categories, and the contract reference. This is the input to the client's section 17 assessment. If you cannot produce it in an hour, the build is not ready to hand over.

## Penalties

### PIPEDA

The OPC **cannot fine you**. It investigates, issues non-binding reports of findings, publishes them, and can enter compliance agreements under [section 17.1](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html). The teeth are elsewhere:

- **Federal Court remedies**, [section 16](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html): the Court may order an organization to correct its practices, order it to publish a notice of the corrective action, and "award damages to the complainant, including damages for any humiliation that the complainant has suffered". A complainant must apply within one year of the OPC report ([section 14(2)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html)).
- **Offence**, [section 28](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/FullText.html): an organization that **knowingly** contravenes subsection 8(8) (destroying information subject to a request), section 10.1 (breach report), subsection 10.3(1) (breach records), or subsection 27.1(1) (whistleblower reprisal), **or that obstructs the Commissioner or the Commissioner's delegate** in investigating a complaint or conducting an audit, is liable on summary conviction to a fine up to **$10,000**, or on indictment to a fine up to **$100,000**. Note what this covers: the fine risk under PIPEDA is concentrated on breach reporting, record-keeping, and obstruction, not on a bad privacy policy.
- Reputational cost is the practical driver. OPC findings are published with the organization named.

### Quebec Law 25

Real money, and the numbers below come from the statute text, not from a vendor summary.

- **Administrative monetary penalty**, [section 90.12](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): maximum **$50,000** for a natural person and, in all other cases, **$10,000,000 or, if greater, 2% of worldwide turnover for the preceding fiscal year**. Imposed by a person designated by the CAI, not by a court.
- **What draws an AMP**, [section 90.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): failing to inform people under sections 7 and 8, collecting, using, communicating, keeping, or destroying personal information contrary to law, failing to report a confidentiality incident to the CAI or to the people concerned, failing to take the security measures required by section 10, failing to comply with the automated decision rules in section 12.1, and specified personal information agent failures. An enterprise may enter an undertaking with the CAI to remedy the failure, and if the CAI accepts the undertaking and it is complied with, no AMP may be imposed for those acts (section 90.1, second and third paragraphs).
- **Limitation on AMPs**, [section 90.10](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): prescribed two years from the date of the failure.
- **Penal fine**, [section 91](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): **$5,000 to $100,000** for a natural person and, in all other cases, **$15,000 to $25,000,000, or, if greater, 4% of worldwide turnover for the preceding fiscal year**. Triggers include unlawful collection, use, communication, keeping, or destruction, failure to report an incident, failure to take necessary security measures, re-identifying a person from de-identified information without authorization or from anonymized information, obstructing a CAI inquiry, and failing to comply with a CAI order.
- **Repeat offence**, [section 92.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): "In the case of a subsequent offence, the fines under this division are doubled."
- **Limitation on penal proceedings**, [section 92.2](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): five years from the commission of the offence.
- **Private damages**, [section 93.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1): where an unlawful infringement of a right under the Act or under articles 35 to 40 of the Civil Code causes injury and the infringement is intentional or results from gross fault, "the court **shall** award punitive damages of not less than **$1,000**". That word "shall" plus a per-person floor is what makes Quebec class actions viable on a breach with a large record count. A 100,000-record incident carries a $100,000,000 floor exposure in a certified class action before compensatory damages.

**UNVERIFIED:** the size and pattern of AMPs actually issued by the CAI to date was not confirmed against a primary source in this review. Do not quote a "typical" penalty figure to a client.

## Common ways sites fail this

- **No privacy officer contact on the site.** Section 3.1 has been in force since 2022-09-22 and it is the single easiest thing for a regulator or a competitor to check from the outside. Sites ship with "contact us" and nothing else.
- **Analytics and pixels firing before the banner is touched.** GTM loaded in `<head>` unconditionally, with the consent gate applied only to some tags. Watch the Network panel, not the tag manager UI.
- **Reject that does nothing.** The banner dismisses, the cookie is written, the pixel keeps firing. This is the failure that gets screenshotted and forwarded.
- **One bundled consent checkbox.** Terms, privacy, and marketing rolled into a single control. Section 14 requires consent to be requested for each purpose and, in writing, presented separately. That consent "is without effect".
- **Copying a GDPR notice and swapping the country name.** The rights differ. There is no general right to erasure in either PIPEDA or Law 25 (Quebec has section 28.1 cessation and de-indexing on stated conditions, which is narrower). There is no PIPEDA portability right. Promising rights the client cannot deliver creates a contract problem on top of a compliance problem.
- **Treating section 9.1 as a cookie rule.** It expressly does not apply to browser cookie settings. Teams cite it for the banner and then leave the product's own sharing defaults set to public, which is the thing section 9.1 actually governs.
- **Skipping the section 17 assessment because "it's just Vercel and Stripe".** Every US vendor holding Quebec personal information needs to be inside the assessment and inside a written agreement. Studios hand over a site with eleven third-party domains and no vendor list.
- **No incident register until after an incident.** Section 3.8 requires a register of all incidents, including the ones with no risk of serious injury. It is built before launch or it is built under pressure with gaps.
- **Portability treated as a nice-to-have.** In force since 2024-09-22. Section 3.3 also makes the export a design constraint of the project, so retrofitting it later means reopening the data model.
- **A 30-day access timer nobody owns.** Silence is a deemed refusal in both laws, which converts an ignored email into a reviewable decision.
- **Personal information in third-party error trackers.** Sentry or LogRocket capturing form values, sent to a US region, with no contract and no assessment.
- **Assuming a US client with Canadian traffic is out of scope.** Cross-border commercial handling of Canadians' personal information brings PIPEDA in, and marketing to Quebec is enough to make Law 25 the safe build target.

## Notes

**Federal reform is stalled, and PIPEDA is still the law.** Bill C-27 (Digital Charter Implementation Act, 2022) would have replaced PIPEDA Part 1 with the Consumer Privacy Protection Act, added a Personal Information and Data Protection Tribunal, and enacted the Artificial Intelligence and Data Act. It reached committee in the House (last committee activity 2024-09-26) and died when Parliament was prorogued on 2025-01-06. [LEGISinfo](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/44-1/c-27) still shows the last recorded stage in the 44th Parliament, 1st session, which is the stage it died at, not a live status.

**A successor is now on the table, and it is not law yet.** [Bill C-36](https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/45-1/c-36), "An Act to enact the Protecting Privacy and Consumer Data Act, to amend the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act and to make amendments to other Acts", was given first reading on 2026-06-15 in the 45th Parliament, 1st session, sponsored by the Minister of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Innovation. As of 2026-07-20 it is at second reading in the House: no committee stage, no Senate stage, no royal assent. Nothing in it binds a client today. Build to PIPEDA as it stands and re-check LEGISinfo before quoting a client on future federal penalties, because the CPPA draft that preceded it carried penalties far above PIPEDA's $100,000 offence ceiling.

**Watch the CAI's consent guidance.** The CAI has published draft guidelines on valid consent, and the reading that matters for the build is its interpretation of section 8.1: the section is drafted as a transparency obligation ("inform of the means available to activate"), and the CAI reads it as requiring the profiling functions to be off until the person turns them on, which is functionally express consent (secondary source: [McCarthy Tétrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/quebecs-law-25-and-cookies-not-so-cookie-cutter)). **UNVERIFIED:** the CAI's own guidance was not read against a primary French-language source in this review, and the McCarthy page was bot-gated on 2026-07-20, so treat the "off by default" reading as the studio's risk position rather than a confirmed regulator holding. Build to the stricter reading. It costs nothing extra once the consent gate is wired properly, and the alternative is arguing statutory interpretation with a regulator that can impose 2% of worldwide turnover.

**Section 8.1 versus section 9.1 is a real tension, and it is settled in the text.** Section 9.1 requires highest confidentiality by default but its second paragraph carves out browser cookie settings. Section 8.1 carries no carve-out. Do not let anyone argue the cookie carve-out in 9.1 excuses pre-consent tracking under 8.1. It does not.

**Quebec language law is a separate obligation on the same build.** The Charter of the French language governs the French-language version of a commercial website aimed at Quebec. It is not in this file and it is not covered by any Law 25 deliverable. Flag it at intake for any Quebec client and get counsel input. **UNVERIFIED here:** the current French-language website requirements and their thresholds were not researched for this file.

**Conflicts and overlaps with other files in this library**

- `casl-canada.md`: email and SMS consent for Canadian recipients runs on CASL, not on PIPEDA or Law 25. CASL consent is stricter and separately recorded. A Law 25 consent record does not carry a CASL consent record. Both are needed and both are audited separately.
- `gdpr.md`: Canada holds an EU adequacy decision for commercial organizations subject to PIPEDA. That does not import GDPR rights into a Canadian build, and it does not mean a GDPR notice satisfies Law 25. The rights sets differ, described above. **UNVERIFIED:** the current status of the EU adequacy review for Canada was not confirmed in this review.
- `data-breach-notification.md`: if that file carries US state breach clocks, do not blend them with the Canadian "as soon as feasible" and "promptly" standards. A multi-jurisdiction incident runs all clocks at once and the shortest one wins.
- `us-state-privacy-laws.md`: several US state laws recognize a Global Privacy Control signal. Neither PIPEDA nor Law 25 names an opt-out preference signal, so honouring GPC is not a Canadian legal requirement. Honour it anyway on any build that also serves US traffic, and note in the privacy policy which signals you honour.
- `coppa.md` and `ca-minors-eraser-button.md`: age thresholds differ (Quebec 14, OPC guidance 13, COPPA 13). Build to the lowest common denominator per audience, and do not tell a client that one age gate satisfies all three.

**Litigation trend to watch.** The section 93.1 minimum $1,000 punitive damages award, combined with Quebec's permissive class action authorization standard, is the highest-exposure item in this file for a client with a large Quebec user base. It does not depend on the CAI acting. Any breach with a plausible claim of gross fault opens it. That is the argument to use when a client resists paying for encryption at rest or MFA on admin accounts.
